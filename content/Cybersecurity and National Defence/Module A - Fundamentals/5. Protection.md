# Information Protection
Distinguished in:

| Concept             | What is protected                                | Who defines the rules         |
| :------------------ | :----------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------- |
| **Privacy**         | The individual’s private sphere                  | Fundamental rights            |
| **Data Protection** | Processing of personal data                      | Legal & Governance frameworks |
| **Secrecy**         | Sensitive information of organizations or states | Institutional authority       |

## Privacy

>[!DEFINITION]+ 
>Privacy refers to the protection of an individual's:
>- Private sphere (personal life, autonomy)
>- Communications
>- Behavioural patterns
>- Identity construction

Privacy protection generally involves:
- Data minimization (only collect necessary data)
- Purpose limitation
- Informed consent
- Control over access and disclosure
- Limitations on retention

> [!CHECK]- GPDP (Garante per la Protezione dei Dati Personali)
> The GPDP is an Italian entity responsible for privacy protection. It covers:
> - Right to prevent third parties from accessing what I perceive as "mine"
> - Right to personal identity

> [!WARNING]+ Role of Technology
> Tech enables:
> - large scale data collection
> - Large scale inference (deducing additional data)
> - Automated profiling
> - Mass surveillance
>   
> But also
> - Encryption
> - Access control
> - Privacy-enhancing technologies


## Secrecy

> [!DEFINITION]+ 
> Secrecy is the protection of sensitive institutional or state information. It differs from security by protecting strategic information which may result in harm in the event of unwanted disclosure.

## Data Protection

Digital systems generate:
- Explicit data (what we intentionally share)
- Implicit data (e.g. logs, metadata, patterns, etc.)
- Derived data (inferences and profiling)

Risk arises from:
- Data aggregation
- Cross-dataset correlation
- Predictive analytics

> [!WARNING]+ Accessible vs Public vs Legitimately Usable
> - Accessible: Technically reachable
> - Public: Intentionally disclosed
> - Legitimately usable: allowed by law and purpose
>
> For example, publicly accessible data does not mean it is ok to scrape it and use it for profiling.

Data protection tries to constrain power asymmetries given by a digital platform's ability to:
- Aggregate data
- Predict preferences, vulnerabilities, intentions
- Influence choices by ranking, nudging and targeting

> [!BUG]+ The "Privacy Paradox"
> People say "I value privacy", while not actively protecting it (accept all cookies, default settings for services, share too much data etc.)
> 
> This happens due to:
> - Information asymmetry
> 	- Users don't know what is collected
> - Bounded rationality
> 	- Costs are immediate, but privacy harms are delayed/uncertain
> - Dark patterns
> 	- Interfaces steer choices (platforms make it harder to deny access to info, such as smaller "deny" buttons, brighter "accept all" buttons etc.)

# Data Protection Framework

> [!Quote] Universal Declaration of Human Rights; Article 12
> "No one shall be subjected to arbitrary interference with his privacy, family, home or correspondence, nor to attacks upon his honour and reputation. Everyone has the right to the protection of the law against such interference or attacks."


> [!INFO]+ GDPR
> The above article then led to the rules for data processing: GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation). It is a European set of law regarding processing of personal data with the goal of harmonization and protection from the digital economy. The key idea is accountability: organizations must prove compliance: it is not optional, and failure to do so will result in civil or criminal offense.
> 
> Its principles become concrete duties in the following:
> 1. Lawful basis and purpose definition
> 2. Data minimization and retention limits
> 3. Access control and protection measures
> 4. Transparency and data-subject rights
> 5. Documentation and incident readiness


### Some Fundamental Rights of the EU
> [!DEFINITION]+ **Article 7: Respect for private and family life**
> Everyone has the right to respect for his or her private and family life, home and communications.

> [!DEFINITION]+ **Article 8: Protection of personal data**
> 1. Everyone has the right to the protection of personal data concerning him or her.
> 2. Such data must be processed fairly for specified purposes and on the basis of the consent of the person concerned, or some other legitimate basis laid down by law. Everyone has the right of access to data which has been collected concerning him or her, and the right to have it rectified.
> 3. Compliance with these rules shall be subject to control by an independent authority.

## Data Breaches
> [!DEFINITION]+
> A security breach that accidentally or unlawfully results in the destruction, loss, modification, unauthorized disclosure of access to personal data transmitted, stored or otherwise processed.
> 
> These can affect:
> - Confidentiality
> - Integrity
> - Availability

Breaches create direct costs (incident response, forensics, legal, recovery) and indirect costs (downtime, loss of trust, lost business). An average data breach for large organizations costs over \$4M.

# Protection Mechanisms
Main methods include:
- Access control, enforces:
	- Authentication
	- Authorization
	- Accountability
- Encryption
	- Protects data stored and in transit
- Anonymization (in datasets)
	- Not linking personal data to the actual individual (e.g. age data stored with no names)
	- Removes or alters direct identifiers
	- Reduces linkability
	- Requires contextual risk assessment
- Pseudonymisation (Replacing identifiers with reversible tokens to reduce risks)
	- Data cannot be linked to the original subject without having other data
- Logging

**Secrecy vs Confidentiality**

| Confidentiality (Security Property) | Secrecy (Institutional Regime) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Part of CIA triad | Legal & organisational framework |
| Protects data from unauthorised access | Protects sensitive state interests |
| Implemented via technical mechanisms (e.g. encryption, access control) | Implemented via classification & handling rules |
| Applies to many systems | Applies to specific categories of information |

Secrecy is required in national defence to protect strategic capabilities, prevent adversarial intelligence gathering and reducing systemic vulnerability, **however** too much secrecy reduces transparency. There therefore must be balance in a democracy.

> [!IMPORTANT]+ Need-to-Know Principle
> Even with clearance and authorization, access to data should be limited to what is strictly necessary for a specific role or mission.
