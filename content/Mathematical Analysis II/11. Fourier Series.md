Let $f(\mathbb{R}\to \mathbb{R})$ be $2\pi$-periodic such that:

$$
f(x)=a_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_{n}\cos(nx)+b_{n}\sin(nx)
$$

Then:

$$
\begin{align}
a_{0} & =\frac{1}{2\pi}\int _{-\pi}^\pi f(x) \, dx  \\
a_{n} & =\frac{1}{\pi}\int _{-\pi}^\pi f(x)\cos(nx)\, dx  & \forall n\geq 1 \\
b_{n} & =\frac{1}{\pi}\int _{-\pi}^\pi f(x)\sin(nx) \, dx & \forall n\geq 1 
\end{align}
$$

> [!TLDR]+ Norm Types
> There are different types of norm:
> - $\|\vec{v}\|_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}|v_{n}|$ - Manhattan Norm
> - $\|\vec{v}\|_{2}=\sqrt{ \sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{n}^2 }$ - Euclidean Norm or Quadratic Norm
> - $\|\vec{v}\|_{\infty}=\max\{ |v_{n}|\  \forall n\in[1,N] \cap \mathbb{N} \}$ - Infinite Norm, or Maximum Norm

We can define norms for a $2\pi$-periodic function $f$:

$$
\begin{align}
\|f\|_{\infty} & :=\sup_{x \in[-\pi,\pi]}|f(x)| \\
\|f\|_{2} & :=\sqrt{ \int _{-\pi}^\pi f^2(x) \, dx  }
\end{align}
$$
---
Let:

$$
\begin{align}
S_{N}(x) & =a_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{N} a_{n}\cos(nx)+b_{n}\sin(nx) \\
\implies\|S_{N}\|_{2}^2 & =2\pi a_{0}^2+\pi \sum_{n=1}^{N} (a_{n}^2+b_{n}^2)
\end{align}
$$

# Riemann–Lebesgue Lemma

Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be $2\pi$-periodic, and let $S_N$ be the $N$-th partial sum of the Fourier series of $f$. Assume that

$$
\|f\|_2 < \infty.
$$

Then:

1. $\forall N\in\mathbb{N}_+$,

   $$
   \|f\|_2^2
   =
   \|f-S_N\|_2^2
   +2\pi a_0^2
   +\pi\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(a_n^2+b_n^2\right).
   $$

2. The series

   $$
   \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(a_n^2+b_n^2\right)
   $$

   is convergent.

3. We have

   $$
   \lim_{n\to\infty}
   \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\cos(nx)\,dx
   =
   \lim_{n\to\infty}a_n
   =
   0,
   $$

   and

   $$
   \lim_{n\to\infty}
   \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx
   =
   \lim_{n\to\infty}b_n
   =
   0.
   $$

# Bessel–Parseval Theorem

Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be $2\pi$-periodic, and let $S_N$ be the $N$-th partial sum of the Fourier series of $f$. Assume that

$$
\|f\|_2<\infty.
$$

Then:

1. 

   $$
   \lim_{N\to\infty}\|f-S_N\|_2=0.
   $$

   In other words, $S_N$ approximates $f$ with respect to the norm $\|\cdot\|_2$.

   > [!WARNING]+ Remark
   >  This does **not** imply that $f$ and its Fourier series are equal pointwise.

2. **Parseval’s identity:**

   $$
   \|f\|_2^2
   =
   2\pi a_0^2
   +
   \pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(a_n^2+b_n^2\right).
   $$---
Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be $2\pi$-periodic and piecewise regular.

Let $S_N$ be the $N$-th partial sum of the Fourier series of $f$. Then, for every $x_0\in\mathbb{R}$,

$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}S_N(x_0)
=
\frac{1}{2}
\left(
\lim_{x\to x_0^-}f(x)
+
\lim_{x\to x_0^+}f(x)
\right).
$$

# T-Periodic Functions

Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be $T$-periodic. The Fourier series of $f$ is defined as

$$
a_0+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\left[
a_n\cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{T}x\right)
+
b_n\sin\left(\frac{2\pi n}{T}x\right)
\right],
$$

where

$$
a_0=\frac{1}{T}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}f(x)\,dx,
$$

$$
a_n=\frac{2}{T}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}
f(x)\cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{T}x\right)\,dx,
\qquad \forall n\geq 1,
$$

and

$$
b_n=\frac{2}{T}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}
f(x)\sin\left(\frac{2\pi n}{T}x\right)\,dx,
\qquad \forall n\geq 1.
$$

All the results previously discussed remain true for $T$-periodic functions.

In particular, **Parseval’s identity** becomes

$$
\|f\|_2^2
=
T a_0^2
+
\frac{T}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(a_n^2+b_n^2\right).
$$
