# Type 1
Let $f:\mathbb{R}^2\to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous and $\gamma:[a,b]\to \mathbb{R}^2$ be regular.

The line integral (of type 1) of f along $\gamma$ is defined as

$$
\int_{\gamma}f\ ds:=\int_{a}^b f(\gamma(t))\,||\gamma'(t)||\ dt
$$

# Type 2
Let $F:\mathbb{R}^2\to \mathbb{R}^2$ and $\gamma:[a,b]\to \mathbb{R}^2$ be regular and set:

$$
\tau(t)=\frac{\gamma'(f)}{||\gamma'(t)||}
$$
The line integral (of type 2) of $F$ along $\gamma$ is defined as:

$$
\int _{\gamma} F\ dl:=\int_{\gamma}F\cdot \tau\ ds
$$

> [!GRAPH]+ Note
> If $\gamma$ is closed, we use the notation:
> 
> $$
> \oint_{\gamma}F\cdot dl
> $$
> 
> This being the circulation of $F$ along $\gamma$

This type of integral DOES depend on orientation, unlike type 1.

# Green's Theorem

> [!GRAPH]+ Jordan's Curve/Jordan's Region
> A simple and closed curve in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is called a Jordan's curve.
> $D\subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ open whose boundary $\delta D$ is the union of finitely many disjoint Jordan's curves is called Jordan's region.
> ![[Pasted image 20260802211854.png]]


$\delta D$ is positively oriented if the region $D$ is always on the left of the oriented curve.

![[Pasted image 20260802212032.png]]

> [!TLDR]+ Green's Theorem
> Let $D\subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ be a Jordan's region with a $\delta D$ positively oriented and piecewise regular and let $F\in C^1(D;\mathbb{R}^2)$. Then:
> 
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \oint_{\delta D}F\cdot dl & =\iint_{D} \text{curl } F\ dxdy \\
>  & =\iint_{D}\left( \frac{\partial F_{2}}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial y}  \right)dxdy
> \end{align}
> $$
> 
> Or negate if negatively oriented.

# Conservative Fields
Let $D\subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$· We say that D is **arc-connected** if $\forall P,Q\in D \exists$ a continuous path joining $P$ & $Q$ contained in $D$.

Same for volumes.

> [!DEFINITION]+
> Let $D\subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be arc-connected and $F:D\to \mathbb{R}^2$ be continuous. We say that $F$ is conservative in $D$ if
> 
 > $$
> \exists U:D\to \mathbb{R}:U\in C^1(D) \text{ and } \nabla U=F \text{ in } D
> $$
> 
> $U$ is called the potential of $F$.


> [!TLDR]+
> A conservative field is one that is radial. i.e. it can be written as:
> 
> $$
> F(\vec{P})=f(\vec{P})\cdot \frac{\vec{P}}{|P|}
> $$
> 
> Where $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to \mathbb{R}$. It also has no curl in its domain i.e. $\nabla \times F(\vec{P})=\vec{0}\ \forall \vec{P}\in D$

Let $D\in \mathbb{R}^n$ be arc connected and $\gamma$ be any regular curve that connects points $a,b$ in $D$. $F:D\to \mathbb{R}^n$ is a conservative function with potential $U:D\to \mathbb{R}$. Then,

$$
\int_{\gamma}F\cdot dl=U(b)-U(a)
$$

So the line integral of a conservative function does **NOT** depend on the path.

If $\gamma$ is closed, then:

$$
\oint_{\gamma}F\cdot dl=0
$$

If $F$ is simply connected (no holes and arc-connected), then:

$$
F \text{ is irrotational}\Leftrightarrow F \text{ is conservative}
$$

While if it is only arc-connected, we can only affirm that:

$$
F \text{ is conservative}\implies F \text{ is irrotational}
$$

But:

$$
F \text{ is irrotational} \not \implies F \text{ is conservative}
$$
