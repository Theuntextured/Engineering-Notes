# Sequences

Let $(a_{n})_{n}$ be an increasing sequence. Then,

$$
\lim_{ n \to \infty } (a_{n})=\begin{cases}
\mathcal{l}\in \mathbb{R} \\
-\infty
\end{cases}
$$

While if it is also bounded, then

$$
\lim_{ n \to \infty }(a_{n})=\mathcal l \in \mathbb{R}
$$

And the other way round is valid, so

$$
\lim_{ n \to \infty } (a_{n})\in \mathbb{R}\implies a_{n} \text{ is bounded}
$$

# Series
There are three types of series (sums of sequences):

$$
\begin{align}
\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{2^n}=1 & \implies \text{Convergent, meaning it "converges" to a real value} \\
\sum_{n=1}^\infty n=+\infty & \implies\text{Divergent, meaning it "diverges" to positive or negative infinity} \\
\sum_{n=1}^\infty(-1)^n \not \exists  & \implies \text{Limit does not exist. It does not converge or diverge to a single value}
\end{align}
$$

> [!EXAMPLE]+ Geometric Series/Sequence
> 
> $$
> a_{n}=q^n\implies S_{n}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty q^n=\begin{cases}
>  &  & \frac{1}{1-q}, &  & |q|<1 \\
>  &  & +\infty, &  & q\geq_{1} \\
>  &  & \not \exists, &  & q\leq-1
> \end{cases}
> $$


$$
\lim_{ n \to \infty } a_{n}=0\implies \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_{n}=l\in \mathbb{R}
$$
## Asymptotic Comparison

Let $a_{n},b_{n}$ be positive sequences ($a_{n}\geq_{0},b_{n}\geq_{0}\forall n\in \mathbb{N}$). Then, let

$$
\lim_{ n \to \infty }  \frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}=1
$$

Then we have:

$$
\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_{n}=l\in \mathbb{R}\Leftrightarrow \sum_{n=0}^\infty b_{n}=k\in \mathbb{R}
$$

## Ratio Criterion & Root Criterion
Let $a_{n}\geq_{0}\forall n\in \mathbb{N}$ and $l\in \mathbb{R}^+\cup \{0\}$.

$$
\lim_{ n \to \infty } \frac{a_{n}}{a_{n-1}}=1
$$

Then:

$$
\begin{align}
l\in[0,1) & \implies \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_{n}=l\in \mathbb{R} \\
l\in(1,\infty) & \implies \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_{n}=\pm \infty
\end{align}
$$

Now let:

$$
\lim_{ n \to \infty } \sqrt{ a_{n} }=l
$$
Then,

$$
\begin{align}
l\in[0,1) & \implies \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_{n}=l\in \mathbb{R} \\
l\in(1,\infty) & \implies \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_{n}=\pm \infty
\end{align}
$$

## Arbitrary Sign

> [!DEFINITION]+
> We say that a sequence has arbitrary sign if it has infinite positive terms and infinite negative terms. 

$$
\sum_{n=0}^\infty|a_{n}|=l\in \mathbb{R}\implies \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_{n}=k\in \mathbb{R}
$$

## Leibniz' Criterion

Consider the sequence:

$$
b_{n}=(-1)^na_{n}
$$



$$
\begin{cases}
\lim_{ n \to \infty } a_{n}=0 \\
a_{n} \text{ is decreasing}
\end{cases}
\implies \sum_{n=0}^\infty b_{n}=l\in \mathbb{R}
$$
