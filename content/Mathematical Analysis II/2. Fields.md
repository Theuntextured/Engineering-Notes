
> [!DEFINITION]+
> A scalar field is defined as $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to \mathbb{R}$ for $n>1$.

> [!DEFINITION]+
Let $f:D\subseteq \mathbb{R}^2\to \mathbb{R}$ and $c\in \mathbb{R}$. The set:
> 
> $$
> S_{c}=\{(x,y)\in D:f(x,y)=c\}
> $$
>
>is called the **c-level set** of $f$. (similar def for $\mathbb{R}^3$). In simple terms, $S_{c}=f^{-1}(c)$

# Distance, Modulus and Radial Functions

$$
||\vec{V}||=\sqrt{ \sum_{k=1}^nV_{k}^2 }
$$

**Distance** between points $A,B$ is:

$$
d(\vec{A},\vec{B})=||\vec{A}-\vec{B} ||=||\vec{B}-\vec{A}||
$$

A **radial function** is one that depends only on the distance of $P$ from the origin $O$, so

$$
\forall P_{1},P_{2} \text{ with } ||P_{1}||=||P_{2}||\implies f(P_{1})=f(P_{2})
$$

# Limits

> [!NOTE]+
> All rules regarding limits from [[Mathematical Analysis I/index|Analysis I]] apply to limits in fields.

A field $f$ is continuous at $P_{0}$ if 

$$
\lim_{ (x,y) \to P_{0} } f(x,y)=f(P_{0})
$$

A field $f$ is continuous in a set $D$ if it is continuous $\forall P_{0}\in D$
