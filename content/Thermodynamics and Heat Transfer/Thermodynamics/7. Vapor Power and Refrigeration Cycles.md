> [!SUMMARY]+ Vapor power plant
> Water is the working fluid. Four steady-state devices: **boiler** → **turbine** → **condenser** → **pump**. Cycle: $\dot{W}_{cycle}=\dot{Q}_{cycle}$, $\eta=1-\dot{Q}_{out}/\dot{Q}_{in}<1$.

## Component balances (SS, $\Delta KE,\Delta PE \approx 0$)

| Device | $\dot{Q}/\dot{m}$ | $\dot{W}/\dot{m}$ |
|---|---|---|
| Turbine (adiabatic) | $0$ | $\dot{W}_t/\dot{m}=h_1-h_2>0$ |
| Condenser | $\dot{Q}_{out}/\dot{m}=h_3-h_2<0$ | $0$ |
| Pump (adiabatic) | $0$ | $\dot{W}_p/\dot{m}=h_3-h_4<0$ |
| Boiler | $\dot{Q}_{in}/\dot{m}=h_1-h_4>0$ | $0$ |

$$
\boxed{\eta=\frac{\dot{W}_t/\dot{m}+\dot{W}_p/\dot{m}}{\dot{Q}_{in}/\dot{m}}=\frac{h_1-h_2-(h_4-h_3)}{h_1-h_4}=1-\frac{h_2-h_3}{h_1-h_4}}
$$

**Back work ratio:** $bwr=\dfrac{|\dot{W}_p/\dot{m}|}{\dot{W}_t/\dot{m}}=\dfrac{h_4-h_3}{h_1-h_2}$ — vapor plants $\approx 1\text{–}2\%$ (much lower than gas turbines).

## Ideal Rankine cycle
Assumptions: no pressure drops in boiler/condenser; turbine & pump **adiabatic + internally reversible** (isentropic); boiler/condenser at **constant $p$**.

| Step | Process |
|---|---|
| 1→2s | Isentropic expansion (sat. vapor → condenser $p$) |
| 2s→3 | Isobaric condensation → sat. liquid |
| 3→4s | Isentropic compression (pump) |
| 4s→1 | Isobaric heating + vaporization → sat. vapor |

On **$T$–$s$**: areas under isobars give $\dot{Q}_{in}/\dot{m}$, $\dot{Q}_{out}/\dot{m}$; enclosed area = net work per unit mass.

**Pump (ideal):** incompressible liquid $v_3\approx v_{4s}$

$$
\frac{\dot{W}_p}{\dot{m}}\bigg|_{rev}=-\int_3^{4s} v\,dp \approx -v_3(p_{4s}-p_3)=h_3-h_{4s}
$$

## Actual Rankine cycle
| Component          | Model                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Turbine            | Adiabatic + **irreversible** → $\eta_{is,t}=\dfrac{h_1-h_2}{h_1-h_{2s}}<1$                                                |
| Pump               | Adiabatic + **reversible** (isentropic); if pump irreversibility is included, $\eta_{is,p}=\dfrac{h_{4s}-h_3}{h_4-h_3}<1$ |
| Boiler / condenser | Internally rev., **externally irreversible** ($\Delta T$ with reservoirs); $p\approx$ const                               |

## Improving $\eta$ (same logic as Carnot: ↑ $T_H$, ↓ $T_C$)
- **↑ boiler pressure** → higher average $T$ of heat addition → ↑ $\eta$, but **↓ exit quality** (erosion risk).
- **↓ condenser pressure** → lower $T$ of heat rejection → ↑ $\eta$, but **↓ exit quality**.
- **Superheat** (1→1′): ↑ mean $T$ of heat addition, ↑ quality at turbine exit, ↑ work.
- **Superheated balances:** $\dot{W}_t/\dot{m}=h_{1'}-h_{2'}$, $\dot{Q}_{out}/\dot{m}=h_3-h_{2'}$, $\dot{W}_p/\dot{m}=h_3-h_4$, $\dot{Q}_{in}/\dot{m}=h_{1'}-h_4$.
- **Reheat** (expand 1→2, reheat 2→3, expand 3→4, condense 4→5, pump 5→6): ↑ quality.

$$
\eta_{reheat}=\frac{(h_1-h_2)+(h_3-h_4)-(h_6-h_5)}{(h_1-h_6)+(h_3-h_2)}
$$

## Regenerative cycle (open feedwater heater)
Extract fraction $y$ of steam after first turbine stage; the remaining fraction $(1-y)$ expands to condenser pressure. Path: $1\to2$ total flow expands, $y$ is extracted to the open feedwater heater, $(1-y)$ follows $2\to3\to4\to5$, then the mixed stream exits as saturated liquid at 6 and is pumped $6\to7$.

Mass balance: $\dot{m}(1-y)+\dot{m}y=\dot{m}$. **Energy balance on heater** (exit sat. liquid at $p_2=p_5=p_6$):

$$
\boxed{y=\frac{h_6-h_5}{h_2-h_5}} \qquad 0<y<1
$$

$$
\eta=\frac{h_1-h_2+(1-y)(h_2-h_3)+(1-y)(h_4-h_5)+(h_6-h_7)}{h_1-h_7}
$$

## Vapor refrigeration

### Carnot ideal (reversed Carnot)
Four fully reversible steps; refrigerant changes phase. **COP** (refrigerator):

| Step | Process |
|---|---|
| 4→1 | Isothermal evaporation at $T_C$ (two-phase), heat absorbed from cold region |
| 1→2 | Isentropic compression to $T_H$ |
| 2→3 | Isothermal condensation at $T_H$, heat rejected to warm region |
| 3→4 | Isentropic expansion back to $T_C$ |

$$
\boxed{\beta_{\max}=\frac{T_C}{T_H-T_C} \qquad \gamma_{\max}=\frac{T_H}{T_H-T_C}}
$$

On $T$–$s$: $\beta=$ ratio of areas (evaporator / net work input).

### Actual cycle (standard)
Departures from Carnot:
- Finite $\Delta T$ with reservoirs: $T_C'<T_C$, $T_H'>T_H$ → $\beta=\dfrac{T_C'}{T_H'-T_C'}<\beta_{\max}$.
- **Expansion valve** replaces turbine (throttling, internal irreversibility): **isenthalpic** $h_3=h_4$. The turbine would produce little work at low quality, while the valve is simple.
- Evaporation to **sat. vapor** (or superheat) — avoids wet compression and liquid droplets damaging the compressor.

| Device | Balance |
|---|---|
| Compressor 1→2s | $\dot{W}_c/\dot{m}=h_1-h_{2s}<0$ |
| Condenser 2s→3 | $\dot{Q}_{out}/\dot{m}=h_3-h_{2s}<0$ |
| Valve 3→4 | $h_3=h_4$ |
| Evaporator 4→1 | $\dot{Q}_{in}/\dot{m}=h_1-h_4>0$ |

$$
\boxed{\beta_{int.rev}=\frac{h_1-h_4}{h_{2s}-h_1}} \qquad \boxed{\beta_{actual}=\frac{h_1-h_4}{h_2-h_1}}
$$

Irreversible compression ($h_2>h_{2s}$) → more compressor work → **↓ $\beta$**. Here compressor work is negative in the device balance, so COP uses its magnitude.

### $p$-$h$ diagram
Use **$p$–$h$ diagram** for refrigerant property work:

- Isobars are horizontal; throttling valve $3\to4$ is vertical because $h_3=h_4$.
- Compressor $1\to2s$ (or $1\to2$ actual) raises pressure and enthalpy.
- Condenser $2\to3$ rejects heat at high pressure; evaporator $4\to1$ absorbs heat at low pressure.
- Inside the dome, constant-$p$ and constant-$T$ lines overlap and quality lines identify the liquid-vapor fraction.

### Throttling & Joule–Thomson
**Expansion valve:** SS, adiabatic, $\dot{W}=0$, $\Delta KE,\Delta PE \approx 0$ ⇒ **isenthalpic** $h_{in}=h_{out}$.

Since $h=u+pv$ is constant, the temperature change depends on how $pv$ changes: if $p_2v_2>p_1v_1$, then $u_2<u_1$ and the fluid cools.

For refrigeration: need **cooling** on expansion ⇒ fluid below **maximum inversion temperature**; sign of $\mu_{JT}=\left(\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial p}\right)_h$:

| $\mu_{JT}$ | On expansion ($dp<0$) |
|---|---|
| $>0$ | $T$ decreases |
| $<0$ | $T$ increases |
| $=0$ | $T$ unchanged |
