## Definition (Clausius)
From $\oint \delta Q_{\text{int,rev}}/T=0$, entropy $S$ is a **state function** (J/K):

$$
\Delta S_{12}=\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\delta Q_{\text{int,rev}}}{T}=S_2-S_1
$$

> [!TIP]+ Clausius inequality
> $$\oint\frac{\delta Q}{T_b}\leq 0 \qquad \oint\frac{\delta Q_{\text{int,rev}}}{T}=0$$
> Equivalently, for a cycle: $\sigma_{\text{cycle}}=-\oint \delta Q/T_b\geq0$.

## Entropy production - closed systems
For any process between states 1 and 2:

$$
\boxed{\sigma=S_2-S_1-\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\delta Q}{T_b}\geq 0}
$$

Convention: $Q>0$ means heat **into** the system. $T_b$ is the boundary temperature; if heat is exchanged with a reservoir, use $T_b=T_{\text{res}}$.

| $\sigma$ | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $=0$ | Reversible |
| $>0$ | Irreversible |
| $<0$ | Impossible |

**One thermal reservoir** ($T_{\text{res}}=\text{const}$):

$$
\sigma=S_2-S_1-\frac{Q_{12}}{T_{\text{res}}}\geq 0
$$

**Several reservoirs:**

$$
\sigma=S_2-S_1-\sum_i\frac{Q_i}{T_i}\geq 0
$$

**Cycle** ($\Delta S_{\text{cycle}}=0$):

$$
\sigma_{\text{cycle}}=-\frac{Q_{IN}}{T_H}-\frac{Q_{OUT}}{T_C}\geq 0
$$

Here $Q_{IN}>0$ and $Q_{OUT}<0$ using the system sign convention.

Rate form: $\dot{\sigma}=\dfrac{dS}{dt}-\sum_i \dfrac{\dot{Q}_i}{T_i}\geq 0$

## Universe / Isolated System
System + surroundings = **universe** (isolated).

$$
\sigma=\Delta S_{\text{univ}}=\Delta S_{\text{sys}}+\Delta S_{\text{surr}}
$$

- **Isolated system** ($Q=0$): spontaneous ⇒ $\Delta S>0$; reversible ⇒ $\Delta S=0$; $\Delta S<0$ impossible.
- $\Delta S_{\text{sys}}$ depends only on end states; $\Delta S_{\text{surr}}$ depends on path (heat is a path function).
- For a reservoir, $\Delta S_{\text{res}}=Q_{\text{to res}}/T_{\text{res}}$. If the system receives $Q_{12}>0$ from the reservoir, then the reservoir receives $-Q_{12}$.
- **Entropy of the universe never decreases.**

## Open Systems
Entropy enters/leaves with mass streams.

$$
\dot{\sigma}=\frac{dS_{CV}}{dt}-\sum_j\frac{\dot{Q}_j}{T_j}-\sum_i \dot{m}_i s_i+\sum_e \dot{m}_e s_e
$$

**Steady state**, single inlet / outlet:

$$
\boxed{\dot{\sigma}=\dot{m}(s_2-s_1)-\sum_j\frac{\dot{Q}_j}{T_j}\geq 0}
$$

## Entropy change — working fluids

### Water (tables)
- Saturation: $s_A=(1-x_A)s_f+x_A s_g$ (kJ/kg·K)
- Subcooled liquid / superheated vapour: read $s$ from tables (Gibbs / Mollier diagrams for visualization).

### Ideal gas - Gibbs equations
From 1st law ($\delta q/m=\delta w/m+du$, $\delta w/m=p\,dv$) and 2nd ($T\,ds=\delta q_{\text{int,rev}}/m$):

$$
\boxed{T\,ds=p\,dv+du} \qquad \boxed{T\,ds=-v\,dp+dh}
$$

These are **property relations** valid for reversible and irreversible processes; only the heat relation $\delta q_{\text{int,rev}}=T\,ds$ requires internal reversibility.

**Ideal gas** (constant $c_v,c_p$):

$$
\Delta s=c_v\ln\frac{T_2}{T_1}+R\ln\frac{v_2}{v_1}=c_p\ln\frac{T_2}{T_1}-R\ln\frac{p_2}{p_1}
$$

### Incompressible substance
$dv=0$ ⇒ $\Delta s=c_v\ln(T_2/T_1)$; constant $p$ ⇒ $\Delta s=c_p\ln(T_2/T_1)$; if $c_p=c_v=c$: $\Delta s=c\ln(T_2/T_1)$.

## Isentropic processes & $T$-$s$ diagram
Internally reversible: $dS=\delta Q_{\text{int,rev}}/T$ ⇒

- $\delta Q>0$ ⇒ entropy increases; $\delta Q<0$ ⇒ decreases.
- **Isentropic:** $\Delta s=0$ (often **adiabatic + internally reversible**; adiabatic alone does not always imply isentropic).

On a **$T$–$s$** plot for a reversible process:

$$
Q_{\text{rev}}=\int_{1}^{2}T\,dS \quad \text{(area under curve)}
$$
