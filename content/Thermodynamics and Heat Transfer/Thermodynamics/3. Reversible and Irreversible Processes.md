> [!SUMMARY]+ Why the 2nd law?
> The 1st law cannot predict **spontaneous direction** (e.g. gas expanding from a tank) nor **maximum work** / what limits real performance.

## Ideal vs actual
- Every system has a **maximum theoretical performance** that real devices cannot reach.
- **Reversible:** system *and* surroundings can return to initial state → best performance.
- **Irreversible:** after the process, system and surroundings cannot both be restored.

## Kinds of irreversibility
| Internal (within system) | External (system ↔ surroundings) |
|---|---|
| Friction | Heat transfer through finite $\Delta T$ |
| Unrestrained expansion | |
| Spontaneous chemical reaction | |

> [!IMPORTANT]+ Definitions
> - **Reversible process:** no internal *and* no external irreversibility.
> - **Internally reversible:** no internal irreversibility; quasi-static; state describable by intensive properties at each instant; passes through equilibrium states.
> - External irreversibility (e.g. finite $\Delta T$ heat transfer) can still be present.

## Thermal reservoir
> [!TIP]+ Thermal Reservoirs
> Large enough heat capacity that its **temperature stays constant** when exchanging heat with another system ($c \to \infty$). Processes *within* the reservoir are taken as **reversible**.

$$
\Delta S_{\text{reservoir}}=\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\delta Q}{T}=S_{2}-S_{1}
$$

States 1 and 2 refer to the **reservoir**. For the main system, use the entropy-production balance in [[Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer/Thermodynamics/5. Entropy]].
