## Internal combustion — air-standard model
Real engines: piston–cylinder, fuel+air or CI. **Air-standard** simplifications:
- Working fluid = **ideal gas** (air), $c_p,c_v$ constant
- Combustion → **heat transfer** from external reservoir
- No intake/exhaust strokes; cycle closed by **constant-volume cooling**
- All processes **internally reversible**; $\Delta KE,\Delta PE \approx 0$

**Geometry:** compression ratio $r=V_{BDC}/V_{TDC}=v_1/v_2$ (Otto) or $v_1/v_2$ at end of compression.

Useful air-standard constants: $R=287\,\text{J/kgK}$, $c_p=1004.5\,\text{J/kgK}$, $c_v=717.5\,\text{J/kgK}$.

## Otto cycle (spark ignition)
| Step | Process |
|---|---|
| 1→2 | Isentropic compression |
| 2→3 | **Constant volume** heat addition |
| 3→4 | Isentropic expansion |
| 4→1 | **Constant volume** heat rejection |

$$
\frac{W_{12}}{m}=c_v(T_1-T_2)<0 \quad \frac{Q_{23}}{m}=c_v(T_3-T_2)>0 \quad \frac{W_{34}}{m}=c_v(T_3-T_4)>0 \quad \frac{Q_{41}}{m}=c_v(T_1-T_4)<0
$$

With $T_1T_3=T_2T_4$ and isentropic $T_2/T_1=r^{k-1}$:

$$
\boxed{\eta_{\text{Otto}}=1-\frac{1}{r^{k-1}}}
$$

↑ $r$ ⇒ ↑ $\eta$; limited by **knocking** (self-ignition).

On the $p$–$v$ diagram, enclosed area = net work output per unit mass. On the $T$–$s$ diagram, enclosed area = net heat added = net work because the cycle is internally reversible.

## Diesel cycle (compression ignition)
| Step | Process |
|---|---|
| 1→2 | Isentropic compression |
| 2→3 | **Constant pressure** heat addition |
| 3→4 | Isentropic expansion |
| 4→1 | **Constant volume** heat rejection |

For constant-pressure heating:

$$
\frac{Q_{23}}{m}=c_p(T_3-T_2), \qquad \frac{W_{23}}{m}=R(T_3-T_2), \qquad \frac{\Delta U_{23}}{m}=c_v(T_3-T_2)
$$

Cutoff ratio $r_c=v_3/v_2=T_3/T_2$:

$$
\boxed{\eta_{\text{Diesel}}=1-\frac{1}{r^{k-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{k}\cdot\frac{r_c^{k}-1}{r_c-1}}
$$

As $r_c\to1$, $(r_c^k-1)/(r_c-1)\to k$, so the Diesel formula tends to the Otto efficiency.

As for Otto, the $p$–$v$ enclosed area is net work per unit mass and the $T$–$s$ enclosed area is net heat added.

## Brayton–Joule cycle (gas turbine)
Closed (or open) cycle: compressor → heater → turbine → cooler. **Air-standard**, $c_p=\text{const}$.

| Step | Process |
|---|---|
| 1→2s | Isentropic compression |
| 2s→3 | Isobaric heat addition |
| 3→4s | Isentropic expansion |
| 4s→1 | Isobaric heat rejection |

$$
\frac{\dot{W}_c}{\dot{m}}=c_p(T_1-T_{2s})<0 \quad \frac{\dot{W}_t}{\dot{m}}=c_p(T_3-T_{4s})>0 \quad \frac{\dot{Q}_{in}}{\dot{m}}=c_p(T_3-T_{2s})
$$

With $T_1T_3=T_{2s}T_{4s}$ and **pressure ratio** $r_p=p_2/p_1=(T_{2s}/T_1)^{k/(k-1)}$:

$$
\boxed{\eta=1-\frac{1}{r_p^{(k-1)/k}}}
$$

↑ $r_p$ ⇒ ↑ $\eta$, but **↓ net work per kg** for same $T_3$ — may need higher $\dot{m}$ for same power.

**Back work ratio:**

$$
\boxed{bwr=\frac{\text{compressor work input}}{\text{turbine work output}}=\frac{h_{2s}-h_1}{h_3-h_{4s}}}
$$

Gas plants: $bwr \approx 40\text{–}80\%$ (vs $\approx1\text{–}2\%$ for Rankine).

**Ideal cycle:** on $p$–$v$, $\dot{W}_{net}/\dot{m}=-\oint v\,dp=$ enclosed area; on $T$–$s$, net work = net heat.

### Actual Brayton
Losses by heat exchange with surroundings are neglected, so compression and expansion are still treated as adiabatic. The main irreversibility is **friction**, which increases entropy; pressure drops in heat exchangers are neglected.

Using positive magnitudes for component work:

- Compressor: $\eta_{is,c}=\dfrac{h_1-h_{2s}}{h_1-h_2}=\dfrac{h_{2s}-h_1}{h_2-h_1}$; $\dot{W}_{c,in}/\dot{m}=h_2-h_1>h_{2s}-h_1$.
- Turbine: $\eta_{is,t}=\dfrac{h_3-h_4}{h_3-h_{4s}}$; $\dot{W}_t/\dot{m}=h_3-h_4<h_3-h_{4s}$.

Irreversible steps often modeled as **polytropic** $pv^n=\text{const}$, $n=\dfrac{c-c_v}{c-c_p}$ and $\dot{Q}/\dot{m}=c\,\Delta T$. For the actual cycle, the $T$–$s$ enclosed area is no longer equal to net work; friction appears as extra compressor work and lost turbine work.

### Regenerative Brayton
If $T_4>T_2$, hot exhaust can preheat the compressor outlet in a heat exchanger (regenerator), reducing fuel/heat input. **Effectiveness:**

$$
\boxed{\eta_{reg}=\frac{h_x-h_2}{h_4-h_2}=\frac{T_x-T_2}{T_4-T_2}<1}
$$

$\dot{Q}_{in}/\dot{m}=h_3-h_x$ (less fuel needed).

## Inverse Brayton (gas refrigeration)
Reverse of ideal closed Brayton.

| Step | Process |
|---|---|
| 1→2s | Isentropic compression |
| 2s→3 | Isobaric cooling / heat rejection |
| 3→4s | Isentropic expansion |
| 4s→1 | Isobaric heating / heat absorption from cold region |

**COP:**

$$
\beta=\frac{\dot{Q}_{in}/\dot{m}}{|\dot{W}_c/\dot{m}|-\dot{W}_t/\dot{m}} \qquad \beta_{id}=\frac{1}{r_p^{(k-1)/k}-1}
$$

- ↑ $r_p$ ⇒ ↓ $\beta_{id}$ (opposite trend to power cycle $\eta$).
- Lower bound on $r_p$: $T_3$ must stay above warm reservoir, $T_1$ below cold reservoir.
- Actual cycle: $T_2>T_{2s}$ and $T_4>T_{4s}$, so compressor input increases, turbine output decreases, and useful cooling decreases ⇒ $\beta_{id}>\beta$.
