Thermal systems can involve energy:
- Storage
- Transfer (to/from surroundings via work, heat, streams of matter)
- Conversion

> [!IMPORTANT]+
> Energy is relative, and we are only interested in changes. Not absolute values.

Energy has two forms:
- **[[#Macroscopic Energy Forms]]**: Ones the system has in respect to the outside reference frame
- **[[#Microscopic Energy Forms]]**: Ones related to molecular activity so they are independent of surroundings.

## Microscopic Energy Forms

**Internal Energy $U$**
- Lumps together all forms of energy
- [[Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer/Thermodynamics/1. Basics#Definitions|Extensive property]]

## Macroscopic Energy Forms

$$
\begin{align}
E_{K}=\frac{1}{2}mv^2 &  & \Delta E_{K}=\frac{1}{2}m(\bar{v}_{f}^2-\bar{v}_{i}^2) \\
E_{P}=mgh &  & \Delta E_{P}=mg\Delta h
\end{align}
$$

Both are [[Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer/Thermodynamics/1. Basics#Definitions|extensive properties]].

---

$$
E=E_{k}+E_{p}+U
$$

$$
e=e_{k}+e_{p}+u=\frac{E}{m}
$$

## Energy Transfer By Heat

Heat is transferred due to $\Delta T$. If $Q>0$, it is transferred TO the system. If $Q<0$ then heat is REMOVED from the system.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Heat is NOT a property, since it depends on the path of the process (it is a path function), not on the state.

It is a boundary phenomenon.

$$
\begin{align}
\int_{1}^2\partial Q=Q_{12}\neq Q_{1}-Q_{2}
\end{align}
$$

> [!TIP] Adiabatic Process
> If no energy is transfered by heat during a process, then it is called **adiabatic**.

---
## Energy Transfer by Work

$$
\begin{align}
W&=\int_{1}^2\vec{F}\cdot \overrightarrow{ds} \\
&=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\dot{W}\cdot dt
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
W>0&\implies\text{Work is transferred FROM the system} \\
W<0&\implies \text{Work is transferred TO the system}
\end{align}
$$

> [!ABSTRACT] Work
> Just like heat, work is a path function and therefore not a property. It is also a boundary phenomenon.

### Gas Expansion

$$
\delta W=p\cdot A\cdot dx \implies dV\propto \delta W
$$

$$
W=\int_{V_{1}}^{V_{2}}p\cdot dV
$$

> [!NOTE]+
> $p$ is a function of $V$ ($p=p(V)$). We should therefore know about the function for the above integral. Also note that the equation is always true but applicable only to quasi-static processes. (ideal).

# Efficiency and Thermodynamic Cycles
> [!SUMMARY] Efficiency
> $$
> \text{Performance}=\frac{\text{Desired Output}}{\text{Required Input}}
> $$
> 
> This applies to different kinds of cycles:
> - Power cycles (direct cycles): Heat input turns into work output
> - Refrigeration/Heat pump cycles (inverse cycles): Power input turns into heat transfer

### Refrigeration Cycles
_Symbol is $\beta$_

$$
\begin{align}
\beta&=\frac{Q_{in}}{|W_{cycle}|} \\
&=\frac{Q_{in}}{|Q_{out}|-Q_{in}}
\end{align}
$$
### Heat Pump Cycles
_Symbol is $\gamma$_

$$
\begin{align}
\gamma&=\frac{|Q_{out}|}{|W_{cycle}|} \\
&=\frac{|Q_{out}|}{|Q_{out}|-Q_{in}} \\
&=1+\frac{Q_{in}}{|Q_{out}|-Q_{in}} \\
&=1+\beta
\end{align}
$$
