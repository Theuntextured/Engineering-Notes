> [!SUMMARY]+ Simple Compressible System
> A system for which electrical, magnetic, gravitational, motion and syrface tension effects are negligible.

> [!TLDR]+
> 
> $$
> \begin{align}
> pV & =mRT \\
> u & =u(T) \\
> h & =h(T) \\
> \Delta E_{K}+\Delta E_{P}+\Delta U & =Q-W
> \end{align}
> $$
> 
> For ideal processes we assume:
> - Quasi-static processes
> - $\Delta E_{K}\approx 0,\Delta E_{P}\approx 0$
> - $\frac{dc}{dT}=0$ (As seen above, this is not true: specific heat IS a function of temperature)
> - $\begin{align}\Delta U=Q-W &  & \delta W=p\cdot dV\end{align}$

**State Postulate:** The state of a simple compressible system is completely specified by using two independent intensive properties.

### Ideal Compressible Fluids
- **Specific Heat at Constant Volume ($c_v$):** You are heating a substance trapped in a rigid, unyielding box. Every single joule of heat goes directly into raising its temperature. This is mathematically tied to its internal energy ($u$).
- **Specific Heat at Constant Pressure ($c_p$):** You are heating a substance inside a cylinder with a movable piston. As it heats, it expands and pushes the piston up. Because the substance exerts energy doing this "pushing" work, you must pump in _extra_ heat to raise its temperature by the same amount. This is mathematically tied to its enthalpy ($h$).

> [!TIP]+ Boyle's Law
> **Empirical Law:** A gas in a cylinder is kept at constant temperature, and pressure is changed by changing volume. We therefore have:
> 
> $$
> \lim_{ p \to 0 } (p \bar{v})=\text{constant}=f(T)
> $$
> Where $p$ is pressure and $\bar{v}$ is specific molar volume ($m^3mol^{-1}$). We can use this to get the universal gas constant:
> 
> $$
> \bar{R}=8.314J mol ^{-1} K^{-1}=\frac{f(T_{tp})}{273.16}
> $$
> 
> Where $T_{tp}=273.16K$ is the triple point temperature of water.

### Ideal Incompressible Fluid
- **Liquids and solids do not compress.** Because their volume never changes, the two specific heats ($c_p$ and $c_v$) are identical. We just call it $c$.
- **Change in Internal Energy:** Just multiply $c$ by the change in temperature.
- **Change in Enthalpy:** It is simply the internal energy change, plus the change in pressure multiplied by the volume.

# Ideal Gas State Equation

$$
\begin{align}
p\bar{v}=\bar{R}T \\
pv=RT
\end{align}
$$
> The bar means that the property is "per mole". e.g. $\bar{v}=v\cdot MM$

### Compressibility Factor
$$
Z=\frac{pv}{RT}=Z(v,T)
$$

The deviation from $Z=1$ tells us how much the behaviour of the gas is different from an ideal gas. It approaches 1 when the pressure is small compared to critical pressure, and temperature is high compared to critical temperature.

### Specific Heats and Specific Enthalpy

$$
\begin{align} 
c_{v}(T)=\frac{du}{dT}  & & c_{p}(T)=\frac{dh}{dT}
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
h=u+pv & =u(T)+RT \\
\implies\frac{dh}{dT} & =\frac{du}{dT}+R \\
\implies c_{p} & =c_{v}(T)+R \\
k & =\frac{c_{p}(T)}{c_{v}(T)}>1
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
c_{p}(T)=\frac{kR}{k-1} &  & c_{v}(T)=\frac{R}{k-1}
\end{align}
$$

> **Constant Volume:**
> 
> $$
> \Delta u=\int_{1}^2c_{v}(T)\cdot dT
> $$
> 
> **Constant Pressure:**
> 
> $$
> \Delta h=\int_{1}^2c_{p}(T)\cdot dT
> $$

# Ideal Gases Processes
## Isochoric Process
_Constant volume_
> [!CHECK]- Graph
> ![[Pasted image 20260520193759.png]]

$$
Q=\Delta U=mc_{v}\Delta T
$$

## Isobaric Process

> [!CHECK]- Graph
> ![[Pasted image 20260520193849.png]]

$$
\begin{align}
Q & =W+\Delta U \\
 & =mR\Delta T+mc_{v}\Delta T \\
R=c_{p}-c_{v}\implies Q & =mc_{p}\Delta T
\end{align}
$$

## Isothermal Process
> [!CHECK]- Graph
> ![[Pasted image 20260520194212.png]]

$$
\Delta U=0\implies Q=W
$$
## Adiabatic Process
(_Quasi-static process)_

$$
Q=0\implies \Delta U=mc_{v}\Delta T=-W
$$

## Polytropic Process

$$
pV^n=\text{const}
$$

$$
\frac{T_{2}}{T_{1}}=\left( \frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}} \right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}=\left( \frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}} \right)^{n-1}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
W=\frac{{\Delta(pV)}}{1-n}=\frac{{mR\Delta T}}{1-n} &  & \Delta U=mc_{v}\Delta T \\
Q=mc\Delta T, &  & c=\frac{{c_{p}-nc_{v}}}{1-n}=c_{v} \frac{{k-n}}{1-n}
\end{align}
$$

> $c$ is the **polytropic specific heat**.

> T_{1}T_{3} & =T_{2}T_{4}  \\
> p_{1}p_{3} & =p_{2}p_{4}

## One-Dimensional Flow
_Application of the [[Laws of Thermodynamics#1st Law of Thermodynamics|1st Law of Thermodynamics]]._

$$
\begin{align}
\dot{m}=\frac{A\bar{V}}{v}=\rho A \bar{V} &  & \dot{V}=A \bar{V}
\end{align}
$$

> [!NOTE]+
> $\bar{V}$ is the average velocity, NOT volume ($V,\bar{V}$). $v$ is specific volume (inverse of density $\rho$).

If we have energy flowing through a control volume into one inlet $i$ and one exit $e$, we have:

$$
\frac{dE_{cv}}{dt}=\dot{Q}-\dot{W}+\dot{m}_{i}\left( u_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{V}_{i}^2+gz_{i} \right)-\dot{m}_{e}\left( u_{e}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{V}_{e}^2+gz_{e} \right)
$$

Or in other terms,

$$
\dot{E}=\dot{Q}-\dot{W}+\dot{m}_{i}(\bar{e}_{i,tot})-\dot{m}_{e}(\bar{e}_{e,tot})
$$
