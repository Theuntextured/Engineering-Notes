# Area of a Surface

Let $F:D\in \mathbb{R}^2\to \mathbb{R}^3$ be a regular surface. The area of $\Sigma:=F(D)$ is defined by:

$$
A(\Sigma)=\int_{D}||N(u,v)||\ du\,dv
$$

Where $N$ is the normal vector of the surface.

# Type 1 Surface Integral

Let $f(x,y,z):\mathbb{R}^3\to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function and let $\Sigma$ be a plane defined by $\Sigma=\bar{r}(u,v)$.

With the above definition of the area of a surface, we can define the type 1 integral of $f$ across $\Sigma$ as:

$$
\int_{\Sigma}f\ dr=\int_{D} f(\bar{r}(u,v))\cdot||N(u,v)||\ du\,dv
$$

> [!EXAMPLE]+
> If $\Sigma$ models a plate with density $f$, then $\int_{\Sigma}f\ dr$ defines the total mass.

> [!NOTE]
> Type 1 surface integrals do **NOT** depend on orientation.

# Type 2 Surface Integral
Type 2 is related to flux of a vector field through a surface. So, let $F:\mathbb{R}^3\to \mathbb{R}^3$ and let $\Sigma=\bar{r}(u,v)$ be regular in a domain $D\in \mathbb{R}^2$. Then,

$$
\int_{\Sigma} F\cdot n\ dr=\int_{D}F(\bar{r}(u,v))\cdot N(u,v)\ du\,dv
$$

Where $n=\frac{\vec{N}}{||N||}$ (i.e. normal unit vector of the surface).

### Divergence Theorem

Let $\Omega$ be a volume with surface $\delta \Omega$. We define a vector field $F:\mathbb{R}^3\to \mathbb{R}^3$. We therefore have:

$$
\int_{\delta \Omega}F\cdot n\ d\sigma=\iiint_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot F\ dx\,dy\,dz
$$

> [!NOTE]+
> $\nabla\cdot F$ is the divergence of $F$.

In simple terms, what escapes (or goes in) the surface (left) must be equal to what is produced (or consumed) inside its volume (right).

## Curl Theorem
_a.k.a. Stokes' Theorem_

Let $\Sigma=\bar{r}(u,v)$ for $\bar{r}:\mathbb{R}^2\to \mathbb{R}^3$ be a  piecewise-regular surface in a positively-oriented Jordan Region and let $F:\mathbb{R}^3\to \mathbb{R}^3$. Then,

$$
\oint_{\delta \Sigma}F\cdot dl=\int_{\Sigma}\nabla \times F\cdot n\ dr
$$

Where $\nabla \times F$ is the curl of $F$.
