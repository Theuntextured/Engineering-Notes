
> [!GRAPH]+ $x$-Normal Domains
> A domain $D\in \mathbb{R}^2$ is called $x$-normal (or vertically simple region) if it can be defined as:
> 
> $$
> D={(x,y):a\leq x\leq b \text{ AND } \alpha(x)\leq y\leq \beta(x)}
> $$
> 
> In simple terms, if it can be defined as the set of points bounded by two vertical lines (at $x=a$ and $x=b$) and two functions of $x$. (i.e. it ain't got no holes or discontinuities)
> 
> $y$-normal domains are the same, but inverted.

Let $D\in \mathbb{R}^2$ be $x$-normal and $f\in C^0(D)$. Then we have

$$
\iint_{D}f=\int_{a}^b\left( \int_{\alpha(x)}^{\beta(x)}f(x,y)\,dy \right)\,dx
$$

Same for $y$-normal

# Change of Variables in Double Integrals
Let $D',D\in \mathbb{R}^2,f\in C^0(\bar{D})$ and $\Phi:D'\to D$ such that:
- $\Phi$ is a bijection
- $\Phi$ is $C^1$
- $\det D\Phi \neq 0$

> [!NOTE]+
> $D\Phi$ is the Jacobian matrix of $\Phi$. $\det D\Phi$ is called the Jacobian of $\Phi$

Then we can substitute as follows:

$$
\iint_{D}f(x,y)dxdy=\iint_{D'}f(\Phi(u,v))dudv
$$

### Examples

**Polar coordinates:**

$$
\begin{align}
\Phi(r,\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}
x_{0}+r\cos \theta \\
y_{0}+r\sin \theta
\end{pmatrix} & \implies \det D\Phi(r,\theta) \\
 & \implies \iint_{D}f(x,y)dxdy=\iint_{D'}f(x_{0}+r\cos \theta,y_{0}+r\sin \theta)r\,dr d\theta
\end{align}
$$


**Elliptic coordinates:**

$$
\begin{align}
\Phi(r,\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}
x_{0}+ar\cos \theta \\
y_{0}+br\sin \theta
\end{pmatrix} &  & \det D\Phi=abr
\end{align}
$$

# Triple Integrals

Objective is to split it into one single and one double integral.

### Integration By Columns

An $xy$-normal domain is one that satisfies:

$$
\Omega=\{(x,y,z):(x,y)\in D \text{ and } \alpha(x,y)\leq z\leq \beta(x,y)\}
$$
For $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3,D\subset \mathbb{R}^2$

Then, $f\in C(\bar{\Omega})$ and:

$$
\iiint_{\Omega}f=\iint_{D}\left( \int_{\alpha(x,y)}^{\beta(x,y)}f(x,y,z)dz \right)dxdy
$$

### Integration By Layers

Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^3$ be a solid and $f\in C(\bar{\Omega})$, with:

$$
\begin{align}
\Omega=\{(x,y,z):c\leq z\leq d,(x,y)\in D_{z}\} \\
\implies \iiint_{\Omega}f=\int_{c}^d\left( \iint_{D_{z}}f(x,y,z)dxdy \right)dz
\end{align}
$$
### Variable Substitution

**Cylindrical coordinates:**

$$
\begin{align}
\Phi(r,\theta,z)=\begin{pmatrix}
r\cos \theta \\
r\sin \theta \\
z
\end{pmatrix} &  & \det D\Phi(r,\theta,z)=r
\end{align}
$$

**Spherical coordinates:**

$$
\begin{align}
\Phi(r,\theta,\phi)=\begin{pmatrix}
r\sin \phi \cos \theta \\
r\sin \phi \sin \theta \\
r\cos \phi
\end{pmatrix} &  & |\det D\Phi(r,\theta,\phi)|=r^2\sin \phi
\end{align}
$$
