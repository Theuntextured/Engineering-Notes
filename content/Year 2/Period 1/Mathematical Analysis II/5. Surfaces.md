# Regular Surfaces
Let $F:D\to \mathbb{R}^3$ for $D\subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ be a parametric surface (injective and continuous). $F$ is a regular surface if:
- $F\in C^1(D;\mathbb{R}^3)$
- $\vec{r}_{u},\vec{r}_{v}$ are linearly independent. (Tangent plane is well-defined)

> [!NOTE]+
> Just like with curves, a surface can be piecewise regular.

# Normal Vectors
$$
\begin{align}
N=\vec{r}_{u}\times \vec{r}_{v} &  & \tilde{N}=\vec{r}_{v}\times \vec{r}_{u}=-N \\
n=\frac{N}{||N||} & &  \tilde{n}=-\frac{N}{||N||}
\end{align}
$$

# Oriented Surfaces
Let $\vec{r}:D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2\to \mathbb{R}^3$ be a regular surface and let $\Sigma=\vec{r}(D)$. We say that $\Sigma$ is an oriented surface if $\forall$ closed curves $\gamma \in C([a,b];\Sigma)$ we have: 

$$
\lim_{ t \to b^- }N(\gamma(t))=N(\gamma(a))
$$
