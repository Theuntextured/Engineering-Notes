## Measurement Complex Realities
* Measurements are never perfect: they are biased (due to systematic effects) and hazy (due to random effects).
* **Goal of Data Analysis:** Identify disturbing factors, eliminate them if possible, and extract the position and dispersion indexes (mean and variance).

## Descriptive Statistics & Visualization
* **Main Indexes:** Mean, standard deviation, max, min, median, and quartiles ($Q_1 = 25\%$, $Q_2 = 50\%$, $Q_3 = 75\%$).
* **Box plot:** Graphically represents the Minimum, $Q_1$, Median, $Q_3$, and Maximum for quick operator/machine comparison.
* **Histogram:** Groups continuous data into categorical bins.
    * *Rule of thumb for number of classes ($n_c$) for $n$ data points*:

$$
n_c \approx \sqrt{n} + 4
$$

 > [!WARNING] Class Sizing
> Having too many classes results in empty bins inside the core part of the distribution.

## Handling Outliers (Exclusion Principles)
* **Never exclude purely on statistics.** Always use expert opinion to investigate the physical root cause before dropping an outlier.

### The Chauvenet Method
* Assigns a conventional probability of $50\%$ to the event of finding exactly one outlier among $n$ tests.
* Probability is split symmetrically between the upper and lower tails of a Normal distribution:

$$
\mathcal{P}(x < x_{li}) = \mathcal{P}(x > x_{ls}) = \frac{50\%}{2n}
$$

* **What to do with an identified outlier:**
    1. **Best:** Repeat the measurement and replace the outlier.
    2. **Okay:** Eliminate the data completely.
    3. **Worst:** Substitute with the average of the remaining data (this artificially distorts the variance and shape).

## Normality Tests
> [!INFO] Why test for normality?
> If variations are solely due to random effects, the distribution will be Normal. Deviations from Normality indicate lingering systematic factors.

### 1. $\chi^2$ (Chi-Square) Test
* A rigorous, numerical test that compares absolute experimental frequencies ($f_a$) with theoretical Normal frequencies ($f_t$).
* Rejects the hypothesis of Normality at a defined confidence level (e.g., $P = 85\%$).
* Evaluated via the test statistic $W$:

$$
W = \sum_{j=1}^{N} \frac{(f_{a,j} - f_{t,j})^2}{f_{t,j}}
$$

* **Degrees of Freedom ($\nu$):** $\nu = \text{Categories} - 3$. (The 3 constraints are mean, standard deviation, and total area equals 1).
* It is a *bilateral* test: rejects distributions that are both too different from *and* suspiciously too similar to the Normal curve.

### 2. Normal Probability Plot (NPP)
* A graphical method where the ordinate scale is deformed so a perfect Normal Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) plots as a **straight line**.
* Cannot rigorously assign a numerical confidence level, but shape reveals the physical cause of the error.

| NPP Shape | Curve Characteristic | Physical / Technical Cause |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Straight Line** | Normal | Only random effects are present. |
| **Hypernormal** | Taller center, lower tails. NPP slope is steeper in the middle. | **"Filtering" or Selection:** Operator manually discarded extreme values, or parts were pre-sorted/selected. |
| **Hyponormal** | Lower center, higher tails (bimodal). NPP slope is flatter in the middle. | **Discontinuities:** Different instruments/operators. **Tendencies:** Continuous wear, temp changes, drift over time. |

*Note: The NPP is often more sensitive to specific structural shape distortions than the $\chi^2$ test*.
