**Probability can be:**
- Objective
	- Classical (a priori)
	- Frequentist (a posteriori)
- Subjective
	- Bayesian

> [!EXAMPLE] Classical Definition
> Classical, or _a priori_, statistics evaluates the likelihood of an event through pure logical reasoning before any physical experiments or trials are ever conducted. It fundamentally assumes all potential outcomes are equally likely, calculating probability simply as the ratio of favourable outcomes to the total number of possible events.

> [!CHECK] Frequentist Definition
> _A posteriori_, or empirical, probability determines the likelihood of an event based entirely on observed data from actual experiments or historical records, rather than theoretical assumptions. Instead of assuming equal odds beforehand, it calculates probability by dividing the number of times an event actually occurred by the total number of physical trials conducted.
> 
> $$
> \mathbb P[E]=\lim_{ N \to \infty } f_{E}=\lim_{ N \to \infty } {\frac{n_{E}}{N}}
> $$

# Combinatorics
### Permutations
_Number of ways of selecting and ordering items._

For selecting any amount (arrangements of a given set of $n$ items):

$$
	\mathcal P_{n} =n! 
$$

For selecting an amount $r$

$$
\mathcal P _{n,r}=\frac{n!}{(n-r)!}
$$

### Combinations
_Number of ways of selecting items, irrespectively of order._

$$
\mathcal C(n,r)=\begin{pmatrix}
n \\
r
\end{pmatrix}=\frac{\mathcal P_{n,r}}{r!}=\frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}
$$

## Conditional Probability

> [!IMPORTANT] Definition
> **Conditional Probability**: $\mathbb{P}(E|F)$ is the probability of $E$ occurring *given* that $F$ has occurred.

> [!EXAMPLE] Machine Fault Logic Example
> A machine $B_{i}$ has chance $\mathbb{P}(D_i)$ to fail. *What is the chance that any fails?*
> * **$A$**: Event "Fault takes place".
> * **$B_i$**: Event "Machine in use is of type $i$".
> * **Conditional Link**: $\mathbb{P}(D_i) = \mathbb{P}(A|B_i)$
### Total Probability Theorem

> [!INFO] I. Applying the III Kolmogorov Axiom
> An event $A$ across a partitioned space is the sum of its mutually exclusive intersections:
> 
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{P}(AB_i)
> $$

> [!INFO] II. Conditional Substitution
> We substitute the joint probability $\mathbb{P}(AB_i)$ using the definition of conditional probability:
> 
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(AB_i) = \mathbb{P}(B_i)\mathbb{P}(A|B_i)
> $$

> [!SUCCESS] III. The Final Theorem
> Combining these principles yields the Total Probability Theorem, allowing you to calculate the overall fault rate:
> 
> $$
> \mathbb{P}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{P}(B_i) \cdot \mathbb{P}(A|B_i) = \sum_{i} \mathbb{P}(B_i) \cdot \mathbb{P}(D_i)
> $$

## Bayes' Formula

$$
\mathbb{P}(B_{i}|A)=\frac{{\mathbb{P}(B_{i})\mathbb{P}(A|B_{i})}}{\mathbb{P}(A)}=\frac{{\mathbb{P}(B_{i})\mathbb{P}(A|B_{i})}}{\Sigma_{k}\mathbb{P}(B_{k})\mathbb{P}(A|B_{k})}
$$
