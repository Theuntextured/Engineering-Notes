## Measurement Complex
Metrological characteristics do not apply solely to the instrument. They apply to the entire **Measurement Complex**: Instrument + Operator + Environment + Measurand.

## Core Metrological Characteristics
| Characteristic | Definition | Key Detail |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Resolution** | Smallest perceptible change in indication. | Analogue: $1/2$, $1/5$, or $1/10$ of division; Digital: max increment. |
| **Sensitivity** | Change in indication / change in measurand. | Only applies to differing units (e.g., $3 \mu V/N$). Direct reading sensitivity = $1$. |
| **Stability** | Metrological properties remain constant over time. | Evaluated by checking zero drift (e.g., scale reading changing with no mass). |
| **Uncertainty** | Dispersion of values attributed to the measurand. | Overlapping uncertainty intervals = compatible results. |

### Vernier Calliper Resolution
For a Vernier scale, the shift $x$ of the zero from the main scale line is found via:

$$
x = 10 \cdot (d_p - d_n)
$$

Where $d_p$ is main scale division and $d_n$ is vernier division.

## Precision vs. Trueness vs. Accuracy
> [!WARNING] Terminology Trap
> Do not mix these up. They describe different statistical behaviours.

* **Precision:** Closeness of replicate measurements to *each other*.
    * **Repeatability:** Same operator, procedure, system, location, short time $\rightarrow$ Ideal conditions.
    * **Reproducibility:** Different locations, operators, systems $\rightarrow$ Real working conditions.
* **Trueness:** Closeness of the *average* of replicates to a *reference/national standard*.
    * Numerically evaluated by its opposite: **Bias** (the systematic error estimate).
* **Accuracy:** Closeness of a measured value to the *true value*.
    * Accounts for total variability (combines both precision and trueness concepts).

## Calibration & Traceability
* **Calibration:** 1. Establishes relation between a standard and indication. 2. Uses that relation to obtain a measurement result.
* **Traceability:** Documented, unbroken chain of calibrations relating a result back to a reference standard.

## Measurement Errors
Error = Measured Value - Reference Value. 

### Types of Errors
1. **Systematic Errors:** Constant or predictably variable. Higher magnitude than random errors.
    * **Correction:** Calculate error mathematically and subtract it.
    * **Compensation:** Counteract via physical phenomena (e.g., making scale and workpiece from the same material).
    * **Elimination:** Remove the root cause (e.g., placing the setup in a thermostatic chamber).
2. **Random Errors:** Unpredictable residuals. Managed via statistical averaging (Normal distribution).
3. **Measurement Accidents:** Rare, massive errors. Managed via exclusion principles.

### Thermal Expansion Systematic Error
Effect of temperature change ($\Delta T$) on workpiece length:

$$
L = L_0 [1 + \alpha \cdot \Delta T]
$$

### Reading Errors
* Mostly a factor in analogue instruments (Digital reading errors depend solely on resolution or user transcription).
* **Parallax Error:** Caused by observing the scale from a non-orthogonal direction.
    * *Solutions:* Use a blade pointer (appears narrowest when viewed correctly) or a mirror scale (align the pointer perfectly over its own reflection).
