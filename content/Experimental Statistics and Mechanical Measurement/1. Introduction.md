
> [!abstract] Core Concept
> Measurement data is never an absolute truth; it contains dispersion caused by systematic (deterministic) and random (statistical) factors.

## True Value & Uncertainty
* The concept of a "true value" is merely a theoretical simplification defined in the VIM (JCGM 200:2012).
* To be meaningful, measurement results must always be accompanied by an **uncertainty interval**.

> [!info] Evaluating Compatibility
> * **Overlapping intervals:** Results are considered compatible; the variation might simply be due to random errors.
> * **Separated intervals:** Results are not compatible; it is safe to declare a definitive difference between the measured values.

## Standard Specifications & Tolerances
* **ISO 14253-1:2013:** Dictates how to handle tolerances imposed by external rules, such as laws or contractual obligations.
* **Manufacturer Action:** Must reduce the acceptance zone by adding half the uncertainty range to the lower limit and subtracting it from the upper limit.
* **Customer Action:** Must similarly reduce the rejection zone.
* **Economic Impact:** Shrinking the acceptance zone generates extra costs for the supplier, whilst shrinking the rejection zone generates extra costs for the customer.

> [!warning] Technological Tolerances
> When tolerances are determined internally via testing rather than strict legal obligations, the boundary already accounts for measurement uncertainty. In this scenario, there is no need to artificially reduce the acceptance zone further.

## Quality Rules & Standards
* A measurement result depends entirely on the whole system: the instrument, the operator, the environmental conditions, and the measurand.
* **ISO 9001:2015:** The general quality standard used for factory **certification**.
* **ISO/IEC 17025:2005:** The specific standard used for the **accreditation** of calibration and test laboratories.
* **GUM (JCGM 100:2008):** The definitive guide for evaluating and expressing measurement uncertainty.
