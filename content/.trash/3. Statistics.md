> [!SUCCESS]- Discrete vs Continuous R.V.
> **Discrete:** R.V.s such that the codomain contains only isolated points:
> 
> $$
> \exists\varepsilon>0:(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)\cap \text{Codomain}=\{x\}
> $$
> 
> Examples: Rolling a die/coin. Number of manufactured defective products.
> 
> **Continuous:** R.V.s such that codomains are continuous.
> 
> Examples: Human height, probability of a defective product, temperature on a certain day, ROI.
> 
> > [!NOTE]
> > While n. of manufactured defective products is discrete, on a massive scale (millions of products), we can often treat the data as continuous.

## Statistical Distributions

> [!IMPORTANT]+ Definition
> Function associating to each realization of the random variable a certain probability (or probability density).

### Probability Mass Function

$$
f_{X}(x)=\begin{cases}
\mathbb P[\{X=x_{j}\}],&x=x_{j},j=1,2,\dots,\mathbf{n}.. \\
0,&x \neq x_{j}
\end{cases}
$$

> [!CHECK]+ Rules
> 1. $f_{X}(x):\mathbb R\to[0,1]$
> 2. $f_{X}(x)\geq_{0},\forall x \in \mathbb R$
> 3. $\sum_{j}f_{X}(x_{j})=1$

### Cumulative Distribution Function
_(CDF)_

$$
F_{X}(x)=\mathbb P[\{ s:X(s)\leq x \}]=\mathbb P[\{ X\leq x \}]
$$

> [!SUCCESS]+ Rules
> 1. $F_{X}(x):\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]$
> 2. $\lim_{ x \to -\infty }F_{X}(x)=0$ and $\lim_{ x \to \infty }F_{X}(x)=1$
> 3. $F_{X}(x)$ is monotonous and increasing.
> 4. $F_{X}(x)$ is right-continuous:
>    
>    $$
>    \lim_{ h \to 0^+ } F_{X}(x+h)=F_{X}(x)
> $$

> [!EXAMPLE]+ CDF & Probability Mass Function
> Discrete:
> 
> $$
> F_{X}(x)=\sum_{j:x_{j}\leq x}f_{X}(x_{j})
> $$
> 
> Continuous:
> 
> $$
> F_{X}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^xf(u)du
> $$

## Distribution Parameters
_Parameters of a statistical distribution_


| **Term**           | **Definition**                                                                 | Examples                             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| **Position Index** | Defines the position of the distribution on the axis of the variable examined. | Mean, expected value, central value. |
| Dispersion Index   | How widely distributed terms are.                                              | Variance, standard deviation         |
| Shape index        | Shape of distribution.                                                         | Skewness, kurtosis                   |

> [!CHECK]+ Expected Value/Mean
> > [!NOTE]
> > Expected value and central value are only the same with symmetrical distributions.
> 
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \mathbb{E}[X_{\text{discrete}}]=&\sum_{x_{j}}x_{j}f_{X}(x_{j}) \\
> \mathbb{E}[X_{\text{continuous}}]=&\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}xf_{X}(x)dx \\
> \mathbb{E}[g(X)]=&\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}g(x)f_{X}(x)dx \\
> \mathbb{E}[X]=\mu_{X}
> \end{align}
> $$

> [!SUCCESS]+ Variance and Standard Deviation
> 
> $$
> \mathbb{V}ar[X]=\sigma_{X}^2=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(x-\mathbb{E}[X])^2f_{X}(x)dx
> $$
> 
> > [NOTE] Also applies to discrete values ofc.
>
> $$
> \text{Standard Deviation of }X=\sigma_{X}=\sqrt{ \mathbb{V}ar[X] }
> $$

### Rules

$$
\begin{align}
&\mathbb{E}[c]=c \\
&\mathbb{E}[c\cdot g(X)]=c\cdot \mathbb{E}[g(X)] \\
&\mathbb{E[c_{1}g_{1}(X)+c_{2}g_{2}(X)]}=c_{1}\cdot \mathbb{E}[g_{1}(X)]+c_{2}\cdot \mathbb{E}[g_{2}(X)] \\
&g_{1}(x)<g_{2}(x)\,\,\forall x \in \mathbb{R} \implies \mathbb{E}[g_{1}(X)]\leq \mathbb{E}[g_{2(X)}]
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
&\mathbb{V}ar[c\cdot X]=c^2\cdot \mathbb{V}ar[X] \\
&\mathbb{V}ar[X]=\mathbb{E}[X^2]-(\mathbb{E[X]})^2
\end{align}
$$

> [!IMPORTANT] Statistical Moments
> 
> $$
> \mu'_{n}=\mathbb{E}[X^n]
> $$
> 
> n-th order ***central*** moment of $X$ with respect to $\mathbb{E[X]}$:
> 
> $$
> \mu_{n}=\mathbb{E}[(X-\mathbb{E})^n]
> $$
> 
> $$
> \text{Skewness Coefficient}=\frac{\mu_{3}}{\sigma^3}
> $$
> 
> Alternative Asymmetry index:
> 
> $$
> \frac{\text{average - mode}}{\sigma}
> $$
> 
> $$
> \text{Kurtosis Coefficient}=\frac{\mu_{4}}{\sigma_{4}}-3
> $$
> 
> > [!NOTE] 
> > Normal distribution has kurtosis coeff. = 3
>  
> $$
> \text{Coefficient of Variation}=\text{CV}=\frac{\sigma}{\text{Average}}
> $$
