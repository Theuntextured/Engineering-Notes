## Balance of Rotating Bodies

> [!SUMMARY]
> When the centre of mass of a rotating body does not lie on the axis of rotation, it is said to be unbalanced. An unbalanced rotating system will lead to centrifugal forces with the same frequency of rotation rate. This leads to greater internal stresses, which should be minimized as much as possible.


![[Applied Mechanics/attachments/Pasted image 20260426180645.png]]

In the above example, the centre of gravity is not aligned with the axis of rotation, and therefore goes through circular motion, being subjected to centrifugal force. This is therefore felt by the bearings A and B due to Newton's 3rd Law.

$$
\begin{align}
F_{c}&=m\omega ^2r  \\
\implies F_{A}&=F_{B}=\frac{F_{c}}{2}
\end{align}
$$

The above example assumes that the shaft does not flex during rotation. This however is often not true. If we assume it has a stiffness coefficient $k$, we then get:

$$
\begin{align}
F_{c}&=m\omega^2(r+\Delta r) \\
&=k\Delta x \\
\implies\Delta x&=\frac{m\omega^2r}{k+m\omega^2} \\
\implies \omega_{c}&=\sqrt{ \frac{k}{m} }
\end{align}
$$

![[Applied Mechanics/attachments/Pasted image 20260426182510.png]]
> $\omega_{c}$ is the rotation speed where deflection changes from positive to negative. We can also observe that as $\omega\to +\infty,\Delta r\to-r$, which would lead to $F=0$, and therefore rotational balance. This is called "**Self-centring of hypercritical speed rotators**."
