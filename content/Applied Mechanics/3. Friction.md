> [!IMPORTANT]+ 3 Types
> There are **three** types of friction in applied mechanics:
> - **Dry friction** (Coulumb's friction): Between two contacting bodies with non-lubricated surfaces.
> - **Fluid friction**: Between two contacting bodies with lubricant between the surfaces.
> - **Internal friction**: Occurs as a not perfect flexibility of real bodies when deformed.

### Static Friction

![[Pasted image 20260426185151.png]]

$$
\begin{align}
F_{\text{lim}} & =N \tan \beta _{\text{lim}} \\
 & =\mu_{s}N \\
\beta_{\text{lim}} & =\arctan \mu_{s}=\varphi_{a}
\end{align}
$$

Where:
- $\mu_{s}$ (often $f_{a}$ is used) is called the **static friction coefficient**, or **adherence coefficient**
- $\varphi_{a}$ is the **adherence angle** or **static friction angle.**

---
> [!WARNING]+
> ![[Pasted image 20260426190601.png]]
> 
> In reality, friction is affected by surface roughness, relative velocity, as well as materials. This means that $\mu$ is not a constant (dynamic friction coefficient). Instead, it will vary with $v$, as well as $R_{a}$ ($\mu$ is here represented as $f$).

There are 3 cases:
- $T<\mu_{s}N,V_{R}=0$: Adhesion or stick
- $T=\mu_{s}N,V_{R}=0$: Limit stick
- $T=\mu N,V_{R}\neq_{0}$; Slip. Happens after $T>\mu_{s}N$

## Rolling Friction 

Rolling friction is caused by 3 main factors:
- The rolling object and the surface the object is rolling on are not perfectly rigid, leading to **deformations**.
- Deformations on the bodies are **not perfectly elastic**.
- Surfaces are **not perfectly smooth**, leading to collisions and **micro-sliding friction**.

![[Pasted image 20260426191911.png]]

In the above diagram, $u$ is the **rolling friction parameter**. It is a length which represents the offset for the application point of the reaction force. 

We can then get the dimensionless **coefficient of rolling resistance** $f_{r}$:

$$
f_{r}=\frac{u}{r}
$$

It is therefore possible to get the force required to maintain an object of weight $W$ rolling on a flat surface with rolling resistance $f_{r}$ at constant speed:

$$
F=f_{r}W
$$

# Friction-Based Machine Components
Some mechanical components, such as brakes and clutches operate by using friction. One component is the braking element and the other is the braked element.

> [!IMPORTANT]+ Reye Hypothesis for wear of friction-based components
> 
> $$
> dV = \delta dA=kfp\,dA\,v_{r}
> $$
> - $dV=\delta dA$: volume
> - $\delta$: depth of worn away element
> - $f$: Dynamic friction coefficient, also $\mu$
> - $v_{r}$: sliding velocity
> - $p$: pressure
> - $k$: Constant of proportionality
