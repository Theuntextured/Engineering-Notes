## Types of Forces

| a1  | Concentrated Forces | Can be considered point forces                      |
| --- | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| a2  | Distributed Forces  | Area of effect is non-negligible                    |
| b1  | External Forces     | Applied from the outside of the system.             |
| b2  | Internal Forces     | Forces applied by other components in a system.     |
| c1  | Contact Forces      | Forces caused by contact                            |
| c2  | Body Forces         | Other forces, such as gravity, electromagnetic etc. |

$$
\begin{align}
\vec{F}&=m\vec{a} \\
\vec{M}&=-I\vec{\omega} \\
W&=\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\vec{F}\times \vec{ds}=\Delta E \\
P&=\frac{dW}{dt}=F V+M\omega
\end{align}
$$

> For some fucking reason, the book uses $\vec{Q}$ for momentum instead of $\vec{p}$. Angular momentum, instead is $\vec{K}$.

$$
\text{Impulse}=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\vec{F}_{tot}dt=\Delta \vec{Q}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
&\vec{K}_{P}=\vec{p}\times \vec{Q} \\
\implies&\vec{M}_{O}=\frac{d\vec{K}_{O}}{dt}
\end{align}
$$

> [!TIP] Balance of Rotating Bodies
> When the centre of mass of a rotating body does not lie on the axis of rotation, it is said to be unbalanced. An unbalanced rotating system will lead to centrifugal forces with the same frequency of rotation rate. This leads to greater internal stresses, whuch should be minimized as much as possible.
